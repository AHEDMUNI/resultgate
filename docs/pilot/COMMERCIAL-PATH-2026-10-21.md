## Payment choice

**Use route A first:** customer pays the provider through its existing checkout;
ResultGate receives authenticated conversion and later reversal evidence, computes
eligibility, then reconciles a provider settlement. This avoids building a customer
wallet or funding execution. It still requires contractual/tax review and a real
settlement channel. Do not add a buyer surcharge or provider subscription.

| Route | Verified fees / operating implications | Suitable first step |
|---|---|---|
| A: direct provider commission | No ResultGate payment-processor fee until a settlement channel is agreed. Provider processing, bank/FX fees and minimum payouts are not zero merely because unknown. | Best first commercial route: one provider, one outcome, a signed settlement export if no webhook exists. |
| B: Stripe Connect | US domestic card baseline 2.9% + $0.30. Stripe-priced connected accounts have no additional platform account/payout fees under that model. Platform-priced model: $2/month per payout-active account plus 0.25% + $0.25 per payout, in addition to payment processing. | Later, approved connected-provider onboarding and direct charges/application fees; avoid platform custody and untested automatic purchases. |
| B: x402/CDP | First 1,000 onchain facilitator transactions/month free, then $0.001 each; verify free. Fees count onchain operations, not HTTP requests. Wallet funding, token/network/on-offramp costs and risk remain separate. | Optional later agent payment rail; local simulation now, then explicitly authorized testnet. Not proof of a successful task or commission agreement. |

Sources: [Stripe Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/connect/pricing),
[US processing pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing),
[CDP facilitator](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/seller/facilitator).
CDP supports exact/upto/batch-settlement with differing network support; deposits,
refunds and withdrawals can each count as transactions. Do not assume universal
refund/escrow semantics. CDP authentication and wallet configuration are required;
address screening does not replace a provider's legal identity or agreement.

Illustrative US domestic-card processing (excluding Connect, FX, disputes, tax):
$1 charge costs $0.329 (32.9%); $5 costs $0.445 (8.9%); $10 costs $0.59 (5.9%).
Stripe's [USD minimum charge](https://docs.stripe.com/currencies) is $0.50, so
charging the $0.005671 browser test individually by card is not viable. Prefer
provider billing aggregation, not a new ResultGate prepaid-wallet liability.

[Stripe direct charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges.md?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted)
support application fees and connected-account-scoped payment records. A checkout
completion event can precede final payment; verify paid status and subsequent
refund/dispute events. Bind account, currency, amount, referral and unique transaction
ID; a browser redirect is not payment evidence. Do not accrue fees immediately if
the agreement requires task acceptance or a refund hold.

[Refund pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing) states original processing/Connect/FX
fees generally are not returned. [Disputes](https://support.stripe.com/questions/dispute-fees-faq)
can debit the amount and add region-dependent fees. Allocate loss responsibility
in the agreement; no fixed dispute fee is assumed here.
[Connect verification](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/identity-verification) concerns
people/businesses, representatives and owners—not an AI identity.
[Stripe tax-reporting guidance](https://support.stripe.com/questions/1099-tax-reporting-and-filing-for-platforms-and-marketplaces)
shows responsibilities depend on account/pricing arrangements. Confirm supported
countries, service eligibility, tax collection/reporting and legal entity before
live activation; no eligibility approval has been obtained for ResultGate.

## First-dollar experiment and accounting acceptance

1. Obtain one provider's signed, owner-approved terms including AI-origin referrals,
   exact commission base/rate, success/acceptance criteria, refund maturity, evidence
   format and a settlement minimum that permits a small first payment.
2. Publish an approved discoverable pilot with users' own provider authorization;
   receive a genuinely independent task, never a self-purchase or synthetic customer.
3. Confirm completed work, authorized real payment and matured eligibility; reconcile
   actual provider settlement separately from accrued commission and net profit.

No paid infrastructure is necessary to send the first approved inquiry or run local
sandbox tests. Hosting cannot manufacture commercial consent or independent demand.
For an offline accounting fixture, use integer amounts and immutable adjustments:
paid task $10, hypothetical 10% share -> $1 simulated eligibility; a full refund
creates a -$1 reversal, duplicate receipts add $0, a disputed task stays blocked.
These are chosen test inputs, not a proposed/accepted provider rate or real debt.
An actual Stripe sandbox needs owner-controlled test credentials; it was not run
by this research. No mainnet x402 payment was made.
